This page lists some of the things my [CV](/cv/) leaves out.

In academia we tend to celebrate our successes and hide our failures. As a result, academic CVs are heavily filtered documents: they list what worked but quietly leave out the unsuccessful applications, the proposals that never got funded, and the rejected papers. That filtering is not harmless. It makes careers look like smooth, planned-out sequences of successes, which builds pressure on more junior researchers and hides something that is an integral part of research: failure.

I am publishing this to show some of the rejections, roadblocks, and criticism I have had to deal with. It is explicitly not comprehensive: things are left out to protect colleagues and, sometimes, myself. It is also not meant to say that everything works out in the end if you only try long enough --- some of the things I failed at I eventually managed to bring to a good conclusion, others I did not.

The idea is not mine. It goes back to Melanie Stefan's <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nj7322-467a" target="_blank">A CV of failures</a> (Nature, 2010); the version most people know is Johannes Haushofer's <a href="https://johanneshaushofer.com/Johannes_Haushofer_CV_of_Failures.pdf" target="_blank">CV of Failures</a>.

## Rejected proposals

- 2026: Horizon Europe consortium proposal, not funded

- 2023, 2024, and 2025: the first proposal I ever wrote was reviewed well by the Austrian FWF but not funded in 2023. I resubmitted it to the German DFG in 2024 and again in 2025, was reviewed well both times, and it was still not funded. (I am currently mustering the motivation to pick it up once more.)

## Rejected papers

- 2025-2026: rejected after review by Science Advances --- after making it past the editor (that one stung, and I left the paper alone for a while). Then rejected by the editor at Nature Climate Change. Now published in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-026-03497-w" target="_blank">Communications Earth &amp; Environment</a>.

- 2024: my first attempt at a high-impact journal, rejected by the editors of Nature and of Nature Climate Change. Now published in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-46349-x" target="_blank">Nature Communications</a>.

- 2015: my first PhD paper (that one really hurt), rejected after review by GRL. Now published in <a href="https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-16-4593-2016" target="_blank">Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics</a>.

- Countless critical reviews (as well as many positive and appreciative ones) that still improved my work. Side not with how I deal with them (includes irony): read, be mad about the unjustified criticism, wait a day or two, read again and realize that the criticism is justified, address it as best I can, resubmit.
